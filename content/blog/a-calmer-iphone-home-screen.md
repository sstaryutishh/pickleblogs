Your Home Screen is a small environment you visit many times. Its arrangement can either support the thing you came to do or introduce several other things to consider. A calmer arrangement does not need to be empty. It needs to reflect your intentions.

Set aside ten minutes and work with the apps already on your phone. This is an exercise in choosing what deserves the first glance, rather than a search for a perfect aesthetic.

## Start with three ordinary reasons to unlock your phone

Write down what you most often need: checking the next appointment, sending a message, recording a thought, or finding directions. Pick three that matter in your actual day.

Place the tools for those jobs on the first screen. Keep the order easy to remember. You are designing a small route from intention to action, so a familiar position can be more valuable than an impressive arrangement.

## Move the tempting detours one step away

An app does not need to be deleted simply because it is distracting in the wrong moment. Moving it away from the first screen can make opening it more deliberate.

On iPhone, the App Library provides another way to find installed apps. Apple explains how to [organize the Home Screen and use App Library](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108324). Use that existing structure before creating a complicated system of folders.

The aim is to make your useful route easier to follow. You can still choose entertainment when you want it, without placing that invitation beside every practical task.

## Give each widget a specific job

A widget earns its space when it answers a question you repeatedly ask. “What is next on my calendar?” is a useful job. “Show me more information” is too broad to guide a choice.

Try one widget first. Use it for a few days and notice whether it saves an app opening or simply adds visual noise. Remove it if you are no longer using the answer it provides.

Do the same with badges. Some counts represent obligations; others represent a service’s wish for you to return. Decide which numbers you need to see while doing unrelated work.

## Keep one place to capture a thought

Ideas and commitments often arrive at inconvenient moments. Make your chosen note or task app easy to find so you can record the thought and return to what you were doing.

Avoid deciding how to organize the idea at the moment of capture. A simple inbox is enough. You can sort it during a [weekly review](/blog/a-weekly-review-you-will-actually-do), when attention is available for that kind of decision.

## Try the arrangement before changing it again

Leave the new screen alone for a week. During that time, notice moments when it helps and moments when it gets in the way. If an essential tool is consistently hard to find, move it closer. If an app repeatedly redirects your attention, give it more distance.

The result does not need to resemble anyone else’s phone. It should make your common tasks feel easier and your unplanned detours a little less automatic.
